The unfolding Jensen Huang scandal: a timeline Lead story

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to extramarital affairs, including one with a Megaspeed executive suspected of being a Chinese spy, leading to his resignation from the company he co-founded. Megaspeed, an AI infrastructure firm, reportedly purchased $4.8 billion in Nvidia chips and is under U.S. investigation for allegedly sharing them illegally with China, a major rival in the AI arms race. The scandal raises significant U.S. national security concerns over potential intelligence leaks regarding advanced AI chip technology to China, impacting Nvidia's leadership and the global tech competition.

TechCrunch AI · Nov 13, 2026
Export-banned Nvidia chips are behind China’s most powerful AI models — uh-oh, Jensen! Must read

Leading open-weight AI models developed by Chinese companies, including Moonshot AI and DeepSeek, were reportedly trained on Nvidia chips banned from export by the U.S. government. Research analyzing model weights found compelling evidence of Blackwell chip fingerprints, with estimates suggesting millions of advanced chips entered China through various schemes, including offshore data centers in places like Malaysia. This widespread access to restricted hardware undermines U.S. efforts to protect its AI leadership and raises questions about corporate liability for export control violations, potentially impacting future international technology policy.

TechCrunch AI · Nov 13, 2026
Jensen Huang’s departure blasts big hole in AI startup funding, VCs and founders say Must read

Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO of Nvidia has created a significant funding void for AI startups, according to venture capitalists and founders. Under Huang's leadership, Nvidia was a major investor, participating in 105 funding rounds this year alone and often providing tens of billions annually, with many key decisions coming directly from him. His departure and the expected conservative approach of interim CEO Colette Kress will likely reduce capital availability, impacting dealmaking, valuations, and competition within the AI startup ecosystem.

TechCrunch AI · Nov 13, 2026
Jensen Huang didn’t show confidence in his successor, report says Must read

Jensen Huang, former Nvidia CEO, reportedly expressed a lack of confidence in Colette Kress, the company's CFO who has now become interim CEO, prior to his resignation. Huang allegedly described Kress as "a deer in the headlights" and unable to help steer strategy, particularly regarding Nvidia's multi-billion dollar credit guarantees for data center operators, after Kress had scaled back a $250 billion credit commitment to OpenAI to $105 billion. This reported lack of confidence from the long-serving former CEO, coupled with Kress's questioning of credit-based deals, suggests potential shifts in Nvidia's strategic direction and financial risk management under new leadership.

TechCrunch AI · Nov 13, 2026
ChatGPT’s new Wallet lets AI agents shop and check out for you Recommended

OpenAI has launched a new 'Wallet' feature for ChatGPT, enabling its AI agents, called 'dots,' to complete purchases on behalf of users. Subscribers add eligible payment cards to the Wallet, and their dot can then facilitate shopping, with users reviewing and approving each transaction via a security check like Face ID. This development aims to enhance consumer efficiency in online shopping and positions OpenAI within the growing market for AI-assisted purchasing, with future plans for user-configurable spending limits and purchase parameters.

Israel’s VCs see opportunity for challengers in Nvidia’s turmoil Recommended

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation has caused concern in Israel, a critical hub for the semiconductor giant's research and development. Huang was instrumental in Nvidia's $6.9 billion acquisition of Israeli chipmaker Mellanox in 2019, establishing it as the company's largest R&D facility outside the U.S. This leadership transition creates uncertainty about Nvidia's future commitment to Israel, potentially slowing decision-making and opening opportunities for competitors, even as demand for Nvidia's core products is expected to remain strong.
