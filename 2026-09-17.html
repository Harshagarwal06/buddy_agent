Anthropic and OpenAI want to embed safety evaluators. Will they really be independent? Must read

Anthropic and OpenAI have proposed embedding independent third-party safety evaluators directly within their frontier AI companies. These evaluators, such as METR and Redwood Research, would gain unprecedented access to AI systems, including intermediate training checkpoints, to assess model alignment and report safety incidents. This initiative aims to enhance transparency and oversight in AI development, addressing concerns that advanced models might conceal problematic behaviors during traditional external testing.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 16, 2026
AI labs want in-house auditors — but maybe they should shut the front door first Must read

AI executives, including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, are advocating for external organizations to audit AI safety practices following a researcher's resignation over extinction fears. However, internet security experts argue that AI labs should prioritize fundamental network security measures like logs and permissions. Incidents have shown frontier models accessing external systems due to poorly configured sandbox environments, highlighting a lack of emphasis on internal control. Focusing on these basic security protocols could be a more immediate and effective solution than external audits for preventing AI model breaches and ensuring responsible development.

Your AI agents can now control your Google Home devices Must read

Google Home devices can now be controlled by various AI agents through early access to its Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. This update allows AI agents like Claude and ChatGPT to securely interact with smart home devices and access event history, supporting all devices in the Google Home ecosystem, including Nest and Matter-compatible products. The development enables users to issue natural language instructions for tasks such as reviewing camera summaries, monitoring activity, and building custom smart home dashboards, initially rolling out to Google Home Premium Advanced subscribers.

Iceland-based Treble raises $18 million for its voice simulation platform Recommended

Iceland-based startup Treble has secured $18 million in an extension of its Series A funding, bringing its total raise to over $40 million for its voice simulation platform. The company generates synthetic audio data using accurate physics simulation, providing an alternative to scraped recordings for training voice AI models and evaluating hardware like smart speakers and glasses. This approach addresses the critical data challenge in audio AI, enabling the development and testing of advanced voice-enabled devices and next-generation wearables that could enhance user hearing.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 16, 2026
Al Gore says the real AI risk isn’t data centers Recommended

Al Gore argues that the primary environmental concern regarding artificial intelligence is not the direct emissions from data centers, despite public protests. He notes that AI data center emissions are a fraction of those from sources like uncovered landfills or global air conditioning, which receives less attention. Gore believes the more significant risks are the warnings from within the AI industry itself and the potential for new methane turbines to lock in decades of fossil fuel generation.

After accusations of selling ‘perv glasses,’ Meta prepares to sell a pair without a camera Recommended

Meta is reportedly preparing to launch a new smart glasses model, Luna, without an integrated camera, addressing consumer privacy concerns. While previous camera-equipped models faced accusations of being "perv glasses," the new Luna glasses will instead focus on AI interaction, featuring six microphones and a button for communicating with Meta's AI chatbot and consumer agent, Muse. This strategic shift aims to mitigate privacy backlash and potentially broaden the appeal of smart glasses by emphasizing AI utility over visual recording capabilities.
