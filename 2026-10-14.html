Science Corporation implants its vision-restoring chip in its first Stargardt disease patient Must read

Science Corporation has successfully implanted its PRIMA vision-restoring chip into its first patient suffering from Stargardt disease, an inherited condition that causes central vision loss. The small chip, acting as artificial photoreceptors, is placed in the eye and works in conjunction with camera-equipped glasses to transmit visual signals to the optic nerve. This development offers a potential treatment for Stargardt disease, currently considered incurable, and expands the application of brain-computer interfaces beyond controlling computers to address a range of medical conditions.

Cerebras CEO pushes back on AI doomsday warnings Recommended

Cerebras Systems CEO Andrew Feldman has pushed back against prevalent AI doomsday warnings, stating that some industry leaders should avoid public commentary on the topic. Feldman argues that the focus should shift to AI's immense potential, such as eradicating diseases like cancer, while acknowledging the inherent risks of powerful technologies. He advocates for reasonable regulation and improved community engagement during data center development to foster positive AI growth and prevent negativity from overshadowing its benefits.

Judge orders OpenAI to turn over hiring records in Apple trade secrets case Recommended

A judge has ordered OpenAI to provide Apple with expedited access to hiring records in Apple's trade secrets lawsuit against the AI company. These documents pertain to OpenAI's interviews with former Apple employees who applied for roles in its consumer hardware and robotics groups, ahead of OpenAI's rumored hardware device launch. The court's decision aims to uncover whether Apple's confidential technologies or components were discussed during these interviews, potentially impacting the ongoing legal battle over alleged intellectual property theft.

TechCrunch AI · Oct 13, 2026
Flox Intelligence is building AI to keep wildlife away from trains, airports, and roads Recommended

Flox Intelligence is developing an AI-powered system to deter wildlife from critical infrastructure like train tracks, airports, and roads. Unlike traditional methods such as sirens or fences, this system uses edge devices with perception systems trained on wildlife to adapt its deterrent responses based on the animal's behavior and species. This innovative approach aims to significantly reduce the estimated $272 million annual cost of wildlife strikes to U.S. civil aviation and prevent thousands of train delays caused by animal collisions.

Laplace Intelligence red-teams robots and self-driving cars before they hit the real world Recommended

Laplace Intelligence is developing automated red-teaming solutions to stress-test physical AI systems like robots and self-driving cars before their real-world deployment. The company utilizes AI agents within high-fidelity simulations, reconstructed from real-world data, to constantly probe for weaknesses and potential failure points. This proactive approach aims to discover and fix critical problems in physical AI, ensuring safety and building a foundation of evidence that customers, insurers, and regulators can trust.
