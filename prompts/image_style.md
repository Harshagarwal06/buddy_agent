# News Buddy Editorial Image System

This file is the single visual contract for every article image. The article
changes; the visual grammar does not.

## Fixed frame

- 1184 × 880 landscape canvas with a warm cream paper background.
- One central explainer made from exactly three clear object groups.
- Thick charcoal linework, flat shapes, generous empty space, and one muted
  brick-red or ochre accent.
- The generated artwork is a wordless row of symbolic objects in the upper
  portion of the frame. The publisher adds the numbered three-label legend in
  a clean band below it.
- No photorealism, people, logos, screenshots, UI panels, scenery, gradients,
  watermarks, decorative filler, or model-rendered words.

## Article grounding

Before planning the image, reduce the article to:

1. **Subject** — the concrete system, resource, product category, or actor.
2. **Action or change** — what happened to it.
3. **Consequence** — the result, risk, trade-off, or next state.

Each of the three object groups and each legend label must map to one of those
article-specific ideas. Show the article's central claim or mechanism, not its
broad topic. A viewer should understand why this story matters without reading
the headline.

Do not substitute unrelated generic objects. Do not use `INPUT → SYSTEM →
RESULT` unless those are genuinely the article's three ideas. If the article
does not provide enough information for a grounded diagram, image planning must
fail instead of inventing a scene.

## Layout vocabulary

- **pipeline** — three sequential stages connected left to right.
- **branching** — one source splits into multiple paths or outcomes.
- **comparison** — two opposing states separated by a clear divider.
- **before_after** — an earlier state changes into a later state.
- **bottleneck** — several inputs narrow through one constrained gate.
- **layers** — nested or stacked protection, processing, or control layers.

Choose the layout that explains the article's relationship most directly.
Arrows must express a real causal or sequential relationship from the article.

## Legend

- Exactly three labels, in reading order.
- One to three words and no more than 18 characters per label.
- Prefer concrete article nouns over generic workflow terms.
- Labels are rendered by the publisher, never by the image model.

## Quality gate

Reject the brief if any answer is “no”:

- Can every object be traced to a fact in the article?
- Does the relationship between objects express the article's main change?
- Are the three labels specific enough to distinguish this story from another?
- Does the image still use the fixed News Buddy frame and visual language?
