AI hallucination nearly triggers US military operation Must read

A U.S. military operation against a Chinese vessel was nearly launched due to intelligence hallucinated by an AI chatbot. An analyst used the chatbot to synthesize open-source and classified data, which falsely identified a ship's cargo as nuclear weapons components, leading to military aircraft being airborne before the error was discovered. This near-miss highlights the critical risks of integrating AI into military decision-making, as system errors can rapidly escalate through command chains without sufficient human oversight, potentially leading to international conflict.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 19, 2026
India forces caller-ID apps to feed spam reports to telcos Must read

India's telecom regulator, TRAI, has mandated that caller-ID and call-management apps must share users' spam reports with telecom operators. This new rule requires apps like Truecaller to feed their community-sourced spam data into a blockchain-based platform maintained by telcos for tracking commercial communications. The regulator aims to broaden the pool of reports for action against spammers, though Truecaller views this as an anti-competitive, one-way transfer of commercially valuable data.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 18, 2026
Anthropic is operating a lab that conducts biology experiments Must read

Anthropic has confirmed it is operating a wet biology lab in the Bay Area, utilizing its AI models to conduct physical experiments focused on fundamental biology. This initiative allows AI theories to be tested in real-life settings, with Anthropic also collaborating with external partners and launching a program for vetted bio researchers. The development creates a stark juxtaposition with the company's own dire warnings about AI's existential risks, including bioterrorism, prompting concerns within the tech industry about the potential implications of such advanced capabilities.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 18, 2026
Anthropic’s first embedded evaluator is … Accenture? Must read

Anthropic has appointed technology consulting giant Accenture as its first embedded third-party safety evaluator, marking a significant step in its plan to scrutinize AI models from within. Accenture's AI division, Faculty, will conduct alignment assessments, red-teaming, and test model safeguards, with both companies committing at least $1 billion over five years to the initiative. This move aims to enhance the verifiability of AI safety and address concerns about model misbehavior, though the framework for such evaluations is still evolving.

TechCrunch AI · Sep 18, 2026
A new kind of AI model from a ChatGPT inventor is thrilling developers Must read

TypeSafe AI, co-founded by a ChatGPT inventor, has released Jev, a new transformer-based model that avoids human language to produce "calibrated decisions" instead of text. Unlike large language models, Jev outputs probabilities, making it incredibly cheap, fast, and immune to hallucinations because users pre-define outputs. Developers are enthusiastically adopting Jev for software automation, finding it five to 18 times faster and more accurate than LLMs for tasks like command classification, or significantly cheaper for email classification. This innovation offers a more robust and cost-effective way to integrate intelligence into code, potentially replacing or augmenting LLMs in specific use cases and preventing agent misbehavior.

World model companies are keeping a lot of secrets Recommended

World model companies like AMI Labs and World Labs are operating with significant secrecy regarding their commercialization plans and specific product developments. A startup that builds other startups raised $100M and is all-in on physical AI Recommended

Vantora, a startup lab, has secured $100 million in funding and is shifting its strategy to focus entirely on building physical AI startups for its corporate partners through a proprietary M&A pipeline. This new model allows corporate clients to invest in and directly integrate these ventures into their core businesses, rather than Vantora launching them into the broader market. The change enables Vantora to pursue highly sensitive and strategic physical AI use cases for industrial companies that were previously deemed too proprietary to develop externally.
